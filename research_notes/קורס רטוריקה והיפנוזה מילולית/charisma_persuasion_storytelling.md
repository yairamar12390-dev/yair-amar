# The Science of Charisma, Persuasion and Storytelling for Speaking to Audiences

Notes compiled 2026-11-16 for a staged course on becoming a charismatic speaker. Evidence grades: **strong** (replicated, meta-analytic or large preregistered), **moderate** (several studies, some field/experimental support, limited replication), **weak** (single study, popular book, or theory-only), **contested** (failed replications or active dispute).

Method note: Links marked "(search-verified)" were checked in this session through search results. Links marked "(DOI, from prior knowledge)" are standard citations I did not re-fetch here; the report writer should treat their details as needing a quick check. Several primary-source hosts (hbr.org, iris.unil.ch, homepages.se.edu) were blocked in this sandbox, so some CLT details come from secondary summaries.

## 1. Charisma research: Can charisma be taught, and how?

### Takeaway
Charisma is best treated as a learnable set of signals, not an innate trait. Antonakis's "values-based, symbolic, emotion-laden signaling" approach breaks it into 12 Charismatic Leadership Tactics (CLTs): 9 verbal and 3 nonverbal. Randomized and pre/post training studies show medium effects (d about 0.5-0.75) on how charismatic observers rate the speaker. Other models (Fox Cabane's presence/power/warmth, Pentland's "honest signals") are useful for teaching but rest on weaker or more indirect evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Antonakis and the CLTs (University of Lausanne, HEC)**
- Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti (2011), "Can Charisma Be Taught? Tests of Two Interventions," *Academy of Management Learning & Education* 10:374-396. Study 1 randomly assigned 34 middle managers to training or control, with coworkers re-rating them 3 months later. Study 2 had 41 MBA students give a videotaped speech, receive charisma training, and re-deliver the speech 6 weeks later, rated by independent assessors. The training significantly raised charisma ratings in both. — [UNIL Serval record (search-verified)](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_FDC9DF7BA052); [ResearchGate (search-verified)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258221550_Can_Charisma_Be_Taught_Tests_of_Two_Interventions)
- Effect sizes: mean d = .62. Study 1 (field, managers) d = .47, close to medium. Study 2 (lab, speeches) d = .75, medium. — [Antonakis et al. 2011, UNIL PDF via search snippet](https://iris.unil.ch/bitstreams/bc0be0af-64c6-42a6-b347-6c2f379a8e50/download)
- Managers practiced the tactics for about 30 hours in total. — [Business Insider (secondary; search-verified)](https://www.businessinsider.in/theres-an-invisible-trait-that-makes-leaders-more-effective-heres-how-to-develop-it/articleshow/50387889.cms)
- There are 12 CLTs: 9 verbal and 3 nonverbal. — [People Matters (secondary; search-verified)](https://www.peoplematters.in/article/leadership/can-charisma-be-taught-to-leaders-17045)
  - Verbal: (1) metaphors, similes and analogies; (2) stories and anecdotes; (3) contrasts; (4) rhetorical questions; (5) three-part lists; (6) expressions of moral conviction; (7) reflecting the group's sentiments; (8) setting high goals; (9) conveying confidence that the goals can be achieved.
  - Nonverbal: (10) animated voice; (11) facial expressions; (12) gestures.
  - (This itemized list is from Antonakis, Fenley and Jacquart, "Learning Charisma," *Harvard Business Review*, June 2012. I recalled it from prior knowledge because hbr.org was blocked here. Secondary sources confirmed the 9+3 split.) — [HBR "Learning Charisma" (blocked in sandbox)](https://hbr.org/2012/06/learning-charisma-2)
- Definition and measurement: Antonakis, Bastardoz, Jacquart and Shamir (2016), "Charisma: An Ill-Defined and Ill-Measured Gift," *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior*. The paper defines charisma as values-based, symbolic and emotion-laden leader signaling, and criticizes older questionnaire measures (e.g., the MLQ) as confounded with outcomes. — [DOI 10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-041015-062305 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-041015-062305); mention confirmed in [siliconcanals summary (search-verified)](https://siliconcanals.com/t-research-led-by-john-antonakis-at-the-university-of-lausanne-found-that-targeted-training-produced-a-medium-improvement-in-how-charismatic-people-appeared-to-others-evidence-that-charisma-is-n/)
- Elections: Jacquart and Antonakis (2015, *Academy of Management Journal*) coded 48 convention acceptance speeches (US elections 1916-2008) at the sentence level for charismatic rhetoric. Charisma predicted vote share mainly when the economy was ambiguous; otherwise economy and incumbency dominated. — [Serval record (search-verified)](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_134FF9006BC6); [LSE Business Review (search-verified)](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/72964/1/blogs.lse.ac.uk-When%20do%20charismatic%20leaders%20rise%20to%20the%20top%20job.pdf)
- TED and Twitter: Tur, Harstad and Antonakis (2022), *The Leadership Quarterly* 33(5):101476. More verbal charismatic signals predicted more TED talk views and higher "inspiring" ratings, beyond attractiveness and nonverbal behavior. The same signals predicted retweets on Twitter. — [Serval record (search-verified)](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_C9BAE1F2FED1); [accepted manuscript, CORE (search-verified)](https://core.ac.uk/download/340491883.pdf)
- Prospective meta-analysis: "Virtual charismatic leadership and signaling theory: A prospective meta-analysis in five countries" (*Leadership Quarterly*, online 2021). CLTs improved follower performance (d = 0.52 in person, k = 4; d = 0.21 overall, k = 10) and extra-role engagement (d = 0.19, k = 6). The effect was weaker in virtual settings. These figures come from a secondary citing paper. — [ScienceDirect (search-verified)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1048984321000461)
- Charismatic leadership meta-analysis: Banks et al. (2017), *Leadership Quarterly*. — [ScienceDirect (search-verified)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1048984316302971)
- NLP detection: CLTs can now be detected automatically in text with fine-tuned BERT, which enables automated feedback on speech drafts. — [arXiv 2409.18984 (search-verified)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18984)

**Earlier charisma training**
- Frese, Beimel and Schoenborn (2003), *Personnel Psychology*: a 1.5-day "action training" in charismatic communication (vision, speech techniques, nonverbal delivery) improved managers' charismatic speech in a pre/post design. — [DOI 10.1111/j.1744-6570.2003.tb00157.x (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2003.tb00157.x)
- Israeli field experiment: Dvir, Eden, Avolio and Shamir (2002), *Academy of Management Journal*. Transformational-leadership training for Israel Defense Forces cadets (Tel Aviv University and Hebrew University authors) improved direct followers' development and indirect followers' performance in a randomized field experiment. — [DOI 10.5465/3069307 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.5465/3069307)

**Theory: Weber; Shamir, House and Arthur**
- Max Weber defined charisma as a quality by which a person "is set apart from ordinary men and treated as endowed with supernatural, superhuman, or at least specifically exceptional powers." He saw it as one of three types of authority, typically emerging in crises. — Weber, *Economy and Society* (1922). (Classic text; no link verified.)
- Shamir, House and Arthur (1993), "The Motivational Effects of Charismatic Leadership: A Self-Concept Based Theory," *Organization Science* 4(4):577-594. Charismatic leaders work by engaging followers' self-concepts: they raise the salience of collective identity, link effort to valued identities and values, and increase self-efficacy and collective efficacy. Boas Shamir (1944-2016) was at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. — [DOI 10.1287/orsc.4.4.577 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.4.4.577)
- Charisma as a costly signal, from an evolutionary perspective (Grabo, Spisak and van Vugt, 2017, *Leadership Quarterly*). — [van Vugt publications page (search-verified)](https://www.professormarkvanvugt.com/publications/articles/leadership-and-status/80-charisma-as-signal-an-evolutionary-perspective-on-charismatic-leadership); [Aarhus "Charisma is a costly signal" (search-verified)](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/charisma-is-a-costly-signal/)
- Other Israeli contributor: Micha Popper (University of Haifa) on the psychology of charismatic leaders and followers. (Not verified in this session.)

**Olivia Fox Cabane: presence, power, warmth**
- *The Charisma Myth* (2012) models charisma as presence × power × warmth. It is a practitioner synthesis: the power and warmth components map onto Fiske's warmth/competence research (Section 2), and presence draws on mindfulness. The book's specific exercises (e.g., visualization, "toe focus") have not been tested as a package. Grade: **weak** as a model, **moderate** for its underlying components. (Book; no link verified in session.)

**Alex Pentland (MIT Media Lab): "honest signals"**
- Pentland argues that nonverbal "honest signals" — influence, mimicry, activity (energy) and consistency — predict negotiation, dating, trust and pitch outcomes. Measured with wearable sensors, they predict dyadic outcomes with about 80% average accuracy without using the words. — [Pentland, Interspeech 2011 abstract (search-verified)](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2011/pentland11_interspeech.html); ["To Signal Is Human," MIT PDF (search-verified)](https://hd.media.mit.edu/tosginalishuman.pdf); [MIT Spectrum (search-verified)](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/spring-2009/honest-signals)
- The business-pitch prediction claims appear mostly as illustrations in Pentland's own popular writing, without independent replication. — [Power Cues excerpt (search-verified)](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/power-cues/9781422193600/Morgan_Chapter_05.html)

### Inferences
- The CLTs are the most trainable and best-evidenced backbone for a course. Each tactic can be scripted, drilled and scored on a checklist, and the 2011 effects came from about 30 hours of practice plus video feedback.
- Observer-rated charisma rises reliably with training. Effects on downstream outcomes (performance, votes, views) are smaller and depend on context (e.g., crisis or economic ambiguity increases demand for charisma).
- Shamir's self-concept theory explains why "reflecting group sentiment" and "moral conviction" work: they connect the message to the audience's identity ("we," shared values).
- Evidence grades: CLTs-trainability **moderate-to-strong** (randomized, replicated across samples by the same group, plus a prospective meta-analysis); Pentland **moderate/weak** (strong sensor findings, thin replication for persuasion claims); Fox Cabane **weak**.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the HBR 2012 article or the full 2011 PDF (hosts blocked), so the item-by-item CLT wording and any "percent improvement" claims could not be verified here.
- I found no independent (non-Antonakis-lab) randomized replication of CLT training in public-speaking settings.

## 2. Warmth, competence, status and vocal charisma

### Takeaway
Audiences judge speakers on two fundamental dimensions, warmth (intentions) and competence (ability), with warmth judged first. Vocal prosody (pitch range, rhythm, tempo) is a measurable, trainable charisma channel. "Power posing" claims are largely not replicated and should not be taught as hormone or behavior hacks.

### Cited Findings
- Fiske, Cuddy and Glick (2007), "Universal dimensions of social cognition: warmth and competence," *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 11(2):77-83. Warmth and competence explain both interpersonal and intergroup judgments. High-high elicits admiration; low-low elicits contempt; mixed profiles elicit ambivalence (pity or envy). Perceived status predicts competence; perceived competition predicts low warmth. — [Princeton record (search-verified)](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/universal-dimensions-of-social-cognition-warmth-and-competence/); [APS Observer (search-verified)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/universal-social-cognition)
- Stereotype Content Model / BIAS map. — [Princeton record (search-verified)](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/warmth-and-competence-as-universal-dimensions-of-social-perceptio/)
- Power posing: Ranehill et al. (2015) failed to replicate the hormonal (testosterone, cortisol) and risk-taking effects but did replicate self-reported feelings of power. Simmons and Simonsohn's (2017) p-curve found no evidential value. Cuddy, Schultz and Fosse (2018) counter-p-curve claimed evidential value for postural feedback on feelings. Critics (e.g., Credé) dispute it. Original lead author Dana Carney publicly disavowed the effect in 2016. — [PMC review (search-verified)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6585898); [Metapsychology critique (search-verified)](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/1723); [PeerJ null hormonal study (search-verified)](https://peerj.com/articles/6726); [Cambridge "Powerful postures do not lead to risky behaviors" (search-verified)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/8CB3B773548A4080BC0FE6985E1F7764/core-reader). Grade: **contested** (feelings), **not replicated** (hormones and behavior).
- Vocal charisma (Oliver Niebuhr, University of Southern Denmark): pitch (F0) range matters more than pitch level. Charismatic speech also features more and later-timed high pitch accents, regular rhythm and somewhat faster tempo. — [Frontiers in Communication 2019 (search-verified)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00012/full); [Berger 2024 dissertation, Kiel (search-verified)](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/macau_derivate_00006497/Berger_2024_Dissertation_final.pdf)
- Prosodic training: the PASCAL acoustic-feedback system is used in 12-week and 4-hour courses. In 72 trainees, men improved mainly on pitch parameters and women on duration and phonation. A browser tool reportedly improved six prosodic charisma cues by an average of 53% after one hour (n = 60). — [HEAd'21 paper (search-verified)](https://archive.headconf.org/head21/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/12827.pdf); [Forum Acusticum 2026 paper (search-verified)](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2026/data/articles/000770.pdf)
- Steve Jobs vs. Mark Zuckerberg acoustic comparison (Niebuhr, Thumm and Michalsky, *Speech Prosody* 2018): Jobs's perceived charisma relates to pitch-accent timing and shape, not just higher pitch. — [typeset summary (search-verified)](https://typeset.io/pdf/shapes-and-timing-in-charismatic-speech-evidence-from-sounds-m381ume00z.pdf)

### Inferences
- A speaker should signal warmth early (shared identity, smiles, self-disclosure, audience-oriented language) before or alongside competence (credentials, crisp structure, confident delivery). This matches the CLTs "reflect group sentiment" plus "convey confidence."
- Vocal training is concrete and measurable: widen pitch range, vary tempo, place emphasis late in phrases, and use pauses. Free tools such as Praat can show pitch contours.
- Teach posture as a way to feel and look more confident (feelings-level effect at most), not as a hormone hack.

### Gaps
- Most prosody findings come from a single lab group (Niebuhr and colleagues), with German, Danish and English speakers. Hebrew-language prosody and charisma data were not found.
- Effect sizes for warmth-first effects in live speeches were not found.

## 3. Persuasion science applied to live speaking

### Takeaway
Which persuasion route works depends on the audience's motivation and ability to think (ELM). Framing and moral reframing shift responses when the message fits the audience's values. Cialdini's principles map well to speech design. Inoculation (forewarning plus refuting counterarguments) is a robust, meta-analytically supported tool that speakers can use to protect their message from later counter-persuasion.

### Cited Findings
- Elaboration Likelihood Model: Petty and Cacioppo (1986), *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* 19:123-205. High-motivation, high-ability audiences are persuaded by argument quality (central route), producing durable attitudes. Low-elaboration audiences respond to peripheral cues (speaker credibility, attractiveness, number of arguments, audience reactions). — [DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60214-2 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60214-2). Grade: **strong** for the core distinction; some critics call the model hard to falsify.
- Cialdini's principles: reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity (*Influence*, 1984), plus unity (*Pre-Suasion*, 2016). — [Influence at Work (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/). Grade: **moderate-strong** overall; individual effects vary, and some social-proof and scarcity field effects are smaller than popular accounts suggest.
- Framing: Tversky and Kahneman (1981), "The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice," *Science* 211:453-458. In the "Asian disease" problem, identical options framed as lives saved vs. lives lost reversed majority preferences. Both authors trained and began their careers at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem; Kahneman won the 2002 Nobel. — [DOI 10.1126/science.7455683 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683). Grade: **strong** (replicated in the Many Labs projects).
- Moral reframing: Feinberg and Willer (2013, 2015, 2019 review). People tend to argue from their own moral foundations; arguments reframed into the audience's foundations (e.g., purity or patriotism for conservatives on environmental issues) persuade more. — [Feinberg & Willer 2019 review PDF (search-verified)](https://sdimakis.github.io/moral_psychology/readings/week_10/Feinberg_2019.pdf); [SPC3 record (search-verified)](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/spc3.12501)
- Limits: moral reframing effects are conditional and fairly small. A 2021 preregistered set of three US experiments (N ≈ 1,000-2,150 each) warned that moral framing can backfire by moralizing an attitude and hardening it. — [Social Psychological Bulletin (search-verified)](https://spb.psychopen.eu/index.php/spb/article/download/13053/11779?inline=1). Grade: **moderate**, effects are small.
- Moral Foundations Theory (Haidt and Graham): care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, and later liberty. A 2021 meta-analysis (Kivikangas et al.) supports ideological differences but with weaker loyalty/authority/sanctity effects than originally claimed. — [Kivikangas et al. mention in SPB paper (search-verified)](https://spb.psychopen.eu/index.php/spb/article/download/13053/11777). Grade: **moderate**, with debated measurement.
- Inoculation: Banas and Rains (2010) meta-analysis of 54 cases (about 41 studies, more than 10,000 participants) found d ≈ 0.43. Resistance holds from immediately to moderate delays and decays after about two weeks. — [Communication Monographs abstract (search-verified)](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637751003758193); [typeset PDF (search-verified)](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-meta-analysis-of-research-on-inoculation-theory-3syl0imve3.pdf); [Arizona open textbook (search-verified)](https://opentextbooks.library.arizona.edu/immersivetruth/chapter/inoculation-theory). Grade: **strong**.
- Prebunking (Roozenbeek and van der Linden, Cambridge): "psychological inoculation" games and videos against misinformation. — [Annals AAPSS 2022 (search-verified)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00027162221087936); critique of a 2023 JMIR meta-analysis: [JMIR 2025 letter (search-verified)](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e64430/PDF)

### Inferences
- Live-speaking application of ELM: for an expert, high-stakes audience, invest in strong arguments and evidence. For a general or distracted audience, peripheral cues (credibility markers, confident delivery, social proof such as "thousands have already...") carry more weight. Most audiences are mixed, so do both.
- Two-sided messages with refutation are an inoculation tactic: name the strongest objection ("Some of you are thinking...") and dismantle it. This also signals confidence and fairness.
- Framing: present the same proposal as a gain or a loss depending on the goal (loss frames for prevention and urgency, gain frames for promotion and aspiration). Duarte's "what is vs. what could be" contrast is effectively a framing device.
- Moral reframing: map the audience's dominant foundations before writing, and translate your core argument into their vocabulary.

### Gaps
- Most persuasion effects were measured with written messages in lab or online samples. Direct evidence on live oratory to large audiences is sparse.
- I found no meta-analysis of moral reframing effect sizes.

## 4. Storytelling and narrative

### Takeaway
Stories persuade through "transportation": absorbed listeners counter-argue less, and attitudes shift toward the story. Speaker-listener brain coupling (Hasson) tracks successful communication. Claims that stories release oxytocin and raise trust (Zak) are weakly supported and the wider oxytocin-trust literature has replication problems. Structural tools (Duarte's sparkline, Ganz's public narrative, hero's journey) are practitioner frameworks with plausible but mostly untested effects.

### Cited Findings
- Narrative transportation: Green and Brock (2000), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 79(5):701-721. Transportation into a narrative produced story-consistent beliefs and reduced doubt about story claims. — [DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701)
- Meta-analysis: van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti and Wetzels (2014), "The Extended Transportation-Imagery Model," *Journal of Consumer Research* 40(5):797-817. It pooled 132 effect sizes from 76 articles. Transportation increases story-consistent affect, beliefs, attitudes and intentions, and reduces critical thought. Antecedents include identifiable characters, an imaginable plot and verisimilitude (realism). Receiver factors include familiarity, attention, transportability (fantasy ability), education and female sex. — [EconPapers (search-verified)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/673383); [City Research Online (search-verified)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/); [EurekAlert (search-verified)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/611188). Grade: **strong**.
- Neural coupling: Stephens, Silbert and Hasson (2010), *PNAS* 107(32):14425-14430. In fMRI, listeners' brain activity was coupled with the speaker's, usually with a delay. Some areas showed anticipatory coupling, and the amount of anticipatory coupling predicted comprehension. Coupling disappeared when communication failed (e.g., a story told in an unknown language). Regions included auditory cortex, superior temporal gyrus, angular gyrus and temporoparietal junction. — [PMC2922522 (search-verified)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2922522); [Hasson Lab (search-verified)](https://hassonlab.princeton.edu/document/511). Follow-up: Silbert et al. (2014), *PNAS*. — [PMC4217461 (search-verified)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4217461). Uri Hasson is Israeli-born and did his doctoral work at the Weizmann Institute of Science (from prior knowledge, not verified in session). Grade: **moderate** (small fMRI samples; replicated conceptually in later intersubject-correlation work).
- Oxytocin and story: Paul Zak (Claremont) claims that character-driven stories with dramatic arcs increase oxytocin and later donations. Grade: **weak/contested.** The broader oxytocin-trust base: Nave, Camerer and McCullough (2015), *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, concluded that cumulative evidence does not robustly show that oxytocin causes or reliably relates to trust; plasma measurement is unreliable; underpowered studies dominate. — [PubMed 26581735 (search-verified)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26581735/); [Caltech record (search-verified)](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/rj5sw-bsb59)
- Lane et al. (2015, *PLOS ONE*): a well-powered failed replication of an intranasal oxytocin trust effect. Lane et al. (2016): the IN-OT literature is underpowered and inflated by publication bias. — [PLOS ONE (search-verified)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article%3Fid=10.1371/journal.pone.0137000); [Lane et al. 2016 PDF (search-verified)](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/bias/publication/2016-lane.pdf). The original Kosfeld/Zak et al. (2005) paper kept being widely cited despite non-replication. — [Gelman blog (search-verified)](https://andrewgelman.com/2018/10/24/study-fails-replicate-continues-get-referenced-no-problems-communication-channels-blocked/)
- Duarte's sparkline (*Resonate*, 2010): great speeches oscillate between "what is" (status quo) and "what could be" (the future), ending with a "new bliss" and call to action. Her mapping of King's "I Have a Dream" shows rapid what-is/what-could-be alternation, heavy repetition and three crescendos. — [SuperSummary (secondary; search-verified)](https://www.supersummary.com/resonate/summary/); [Architect Elevator (search-verified)](https://architectelevator.com/strategy/presenting-waveform/). Grade: **weak** (descriptive analysis of selected speeches; no controlled tests).
- Ganz's public narrative (Harvard Kennedy School): Story of Self (why I am called; a choice point, challenge and outcome), Story of Us (shared values and experiences that call the group), Story of Now (urgent challenge, hope and a concrete action). He frames it as leadership practice, learned by telling, listening and reflecting rather than scripting. — [Ganz 2009 worksheet, Harvard DASH (search-verified)](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/30760283); [Leading Change Network participant guide 2024 (search-verified)](https://leadingchangenetwork.org/app/uploads/2024/09/Public-Narrative-Workshop-Participant-Guide_English_2024.pdf); [Commons Library (search-verified)](https://commonslibrary.org/the-power-of-story-the-story-of-self-us-and-now/). Grade: **weak-moderate** (widely used in organizing, e.g., Obama 2008 campaign training; little controlled outcome research).
- Hero's journey (Campbell 1949; Vogler): departure, ordeal, return with the "elixir." For speaking: the audience is the hero and the speaker the mentor (a Duarte theme). Grade: **weak** (literary framework, not empirically tested for persuasion). (No link verified.)

### Inferences
- Design stories for transportation: a specific, identifiable character; concrete sensory detail; a realistic setting; tension and a turn. Avoid breaking immersion with statistics mid-story; place data before or after.
- Hasson's work suggests that the listener's ability to predict (anticipatory coupling) matters. Clear structure, signposting and familiar frames help audiences "sync."
- Teach Zak's oxytocin narrative only as a hypothesis, not a mechanism. The safe claim is that dramatic arcs increase attention and emotional engagement.
- Ganz's Self-Us-Now is an ideal capstone format because it combines CLTs (stories, moral conviction, group sentiment, high goals, confidence) with narrative structure.

### Gaps
- No controlled trials comparing sparkline-structured vs. conventional speeches were found.
- Hasson's coupling studies used recorded stories, not live audiences. Live hyperscanning (EEG or fNIRS) evidence was not verified here.

## 5. Emotion and the audience: contagion, mirroring, humor, vulnerability

### Takeaway
Emotions spread from speaker to audience through mimicry and feedback (emotional contagion), so the speaker's genuinely felt emotion is a lever. Humor raises status when it lands and lowers it when it fails or offends. Disparaging humor harms trust. Vulnerability helps when paired with competence.

### Cited Findings
- Emotional contagion: Hatfield, Cacioppo and Rapson (1993), "Emotional Contagion," *Current Directions in Psychological Science* 2(3):96-99. People automatically mimic the expressions, voices and postures of others, and the feedback from that mimicry shapes their own feelings. — [DOI 10.1111/1467-8721.ep10770953 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8721.ep10770953). Grade: **moderate-strong** for mimicry and contagion. The facial-feedback component is **contested**: the 2016 multi-lab replication of Strack's pen study failed, though Coles et al.'s 2022 Many Smiles collaboration found small effects of posed smiles.
- Mirror neurons as an explanation for audience empathy are widely overstated in popular writing. Grade: **contested** as a mechanism for audience emotion. (No link verified; flag for the writer.)
- Humor and status: Bitterly, Brooks and Schweitzer (2017), "Risky Business: When Humor Increases and Decreases Status," *JPSP* 112(3):431-455. Across 8 experiments, successful humor signaled confidence and competence and raised status. Inappropriate or failed jokes lowered status. — [Wharton PDF (search-verified)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/2016-54537-001.pdf); [Knowledge@Wharton (search-verified)](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/using-humor-in-the-office-when-it-works-when-it-backfires/). Grade: **moderate**.
- "Downward-punching" disparagement humor harms impressions and trust (*Humor*, 2024). — [De Gruyter (search-verified)](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/humor-2023-0158/html)
- Vulnerability: Bruch, Korn and Gollwitzer's "beautiful mess effect" (2018, *JPSP*) found that people judge their own vulnerability more negatively than observers do. The pratfall effect (Aronson et al. 1966) suggests a blunder humanizes a competent person but hurts a less competent one. — [DOI 10.1037/pspi0000120 (DOI, from prior knowledge)](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000120). Grade: **moderate**; the pratfall effect is old and has mixed replications.

### Inferences
- Speakers should feel the emotion they want to evoke (e.g., by recalling a real memory before a story), since audiences catch expressed emotion through voice and face. This matches the CLT nonverbals (animated voice, facial expression, gestures).
- Humor: rehearse and test jokes; prefer self-deprecating or observational humor over disparagement; a failed joke needs a quick, confident recovery.
- Vulnerability works best after competence is established ("pratfall" logic): show credentials or mastery first, then reveal a struggle in the Story of Self.

### Gaps
- No studies were found that directly measure audience-wide emotional contagion in live speeches with physiological measures (beyond small studies), so the claim rests on interpersonal research.

## 6. Concrete, trainable techniques and exercises (derived from the research above)

### Takeaway
A staged curriculum can move from mechanical drilling of CLTs and vocal prosody, through warmth/competence calibration and persuasion architecture, to integrated narrative speeches (Self-Us-Now) with video feedback and observer ratings. This mirrors the 2011 intervention (instruction, practice, videotaped feedback, about 30 hours).

### Cited Findings (basis for each exercise)
- Video-recorded speech, training, re-delivery and independent rating was the format that produced d = .75 — [Antonakis et al. 2011, Serval](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_FDC9DF7BA052)
- Acoustic feedback training improved prosodic charisma cues in hours to weeks — [HEAd'21 (search-verified)](https://archive.headconf.org/head21/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/12827.pdf)
- Ganz's method is learned by telling, listening and reflecting in small groups with coaching — [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/30760283)
- Verbal CLT density predicts TED views and inspiration ratings — [Tur et al. 2022](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_C9BAE1F2FED1)

### Inferences (proposed exercises; these are design suggestions, not tested protocols)
- **Stage 1 – CLT drill (verbal).** Take a 3-minute neutral talk. Rewrite it adding one of each of the 9 verbal CLTs (a metaphor, an anecdote, a contrast, a rhetorical question, a three-part list, a moral-conviction statement, a "we feel" group-sentiment line, a high goal, a confidence statement). A partner counts CLTs per minute. Re-record and compare before and after. Optional: run the text through a CLT detector (cf. arXiv 2409.18984).
- **Stage 1 – Nonverbal amplification.** Deliver the same paragraph three times: flat, "natural," and exaggerated (voice, face, gestures). Viewers rate charisma. Settle at the highest-rated level that still feels authentic.
- **Stage 2 – Prosody lab.** Record yourself and view the pitch contour in Praat. Targets: wider F0 range, emphasis on key words late in phrases, strategic pauses of 1-2 seconds before key lines, and tempo variation (faster in narration, slower at climaxes). Track over weeks (cf. Niebuhr).
- **Stage 2 – Warmth-then-competence opening.** Write two 60-second openings: warmth-first (shared experience, "we," a smile, a small self-disclosure) and competence-first (credentials, data). Test both on peers and rate warmth and competence (Fiske dimensions).
- **Stage 3 – Audience mapping (ELM plus moral foundations).** Before each talk, profile the audience's motivation and ability (central vs. peripheral route) and dominant moral foundations. Write one key argument in two moral vocabularies.
- **Stage 3 – Inoculation block.** Insert a "you may hear..." segment: forewarn, state the strongest counterargument, refute it. Re-test audience resistance with a later counter-message.
- **Stage 3 – Framing switch.** Rewrite a call to action as a gain frame and a loss frame. Discuss which suits the goal (promotion vs. prevention).
- **Stage 4 – Transportation story.** Craft a 2-minute story with a named character, concrete sensory detail, a conflict, a turning point and a moral. Listeners rate transportation using the Green & Brock scale items. Remove any element that breaks immersion.
- **Stage 4 – Sparkline mapping.** Chart your speech: mark each paragraph as "what is" or "what could be." Aim for repeated oscillation and a final "new bliss" with a call to action. Compare with King's "I Have a Dream."
- **Stage 4 – Humor testing.** Pre-test jokes on small groups. Keep only those that land, with no disparaging humor. Practice a recovery line for a joke that fails.
- **Stage 5 – Capstone: Public Narrative (Self, Us, Now).** A 5-7 minute speech built on the Ganz worksheet, with peer coaching rounds. Score it on a CLT checklist, prosody metrics, transportation ratings and charisma ratings by independent viewers. Repeat 4-6 weeks later to measure change, mirroring the 2011 design.
- **Ongoing – Emotional authenticity.** Before emotional passages, recall a real memory tied to the content (method acting). Audiences catch felt emotion through contagion.

### Gaps
- None of the integrated curricula above has itself been tested. Effect sizes come from component studies (CLT training d ≈ .5-.75; inoculation d ≈ .43; narrative transportation meta-analytic effects).
- Evidence on long-term retention of charisma training (beyond 3 months) was not found.
- Hebrew-specific or Israeli-audience studies of charismatic rhetoric were not found in this search. Israeli contributions are mainly theoretical and leadership-related (Shamir; Dvir and Eden; Popper; Kahneman and Tversky; Hasson).
