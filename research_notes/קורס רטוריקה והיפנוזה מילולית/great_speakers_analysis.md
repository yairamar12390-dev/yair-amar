## Q1. World speakers: signature techniques, preparation, collaborators and scholarly analyses

### Takeaway
The best-documented world speakers did not have a natural gift for it. They built the skill through deliberate effort. Churchill drilled himself out of a stammer and wrote his own theory of oratory at 23. Jobs rehearsed for days and reviewed recordings. Kennedy and Sorensen consciously copied Lincoln's short words. King built his sermons from preaching cadence and anaphora, and he could improvise on that structure. Obama co-wrote with Favreau using a "download" session and a binder of his own past speeches.

### Cited Findings

**Winston Churchill**
- He had a stammer and a lisp as a young man. A throat specialist found no organic defect and told him that only practice and perseverance would help. Churchill deliberately looked for chances to speak, which helped him lose the inhibition behind the stammer, though he never fully lost the lisp. — [Finest Hour, "Churchill: A Study in Oratory"](https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-069/churchill-a-study-in-oratory/)
- At 23 he wrote an unpublished article on oratory, "The Scaffolding of Rhetoric." It was published two years after his death. — [Finest Hour](https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-069/churchill-a-study-in-oratory/)
- While serving in the army in India, he read four to five hours a day: Macaulay, Gibbon, Shakespeare, Plato, Aristotle, Burke, Darwin, Malthus and Bartlett's Quotations. This was self-education in vocabulary and cadence. — [Finest Hour](https://winstonchurchill.org/?p=6551)
- He had speaker jitters and planned around them. Richard Langworth documents his strategies. — [Langworth, "Speaker Jitters: Churchill Had Them"](https://richardlangworth.com/speaker-jitters-churchill-had-them-necessitating-strategy)
- One account of his method says he would glance at one sheet of notes, lay it aside, speak fluently for about ten minutes, then take up the next sheet. He prepared thoroughly but delivered as if talking. — [Finest Hour](https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-069/churchill-a-study-in-oratory/)
- The essay's five elements (correctness of diction, rhythm, accumulation of argument, analogy, and wild extravagance of language) are widely taught. Teaching resources that analyze his rhetoric along these lines include the Oak National Academy lesson on structural analysis of Churchill's rhetoric. — [Oak National Academy](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/lessons/churchill-reading-and-structural-analysis-of-rhetoric-75hp6e); [Rhetoricians site: Churchill](https://sites.google.com/view/rhetoricians/home/1900-2000/winston-churchill)

**Steve Jobs**
- Carmine Gallo: "I can't tell you how many hours he put into that presentation, but guaranteed, he put in more rehearsal time than 99 percent of people ever will." Gallo gives no exact figure. — [CIO / Gallo](https://www.cio.com/article/280205/enterprise-software-how-steve-jobs-beats-presentation-panic.html)
- He prepared weeks ahead, held two full days of dress rehearsal before a keynote, rehearsed out loud in full presentation mode, and recorded sessions and reviewed them with his team for feedback. — [Gallo, "Steve Jobs' 6-step rehearsal process"](https://www.carminegallo.com/steve-jobs-6-step-rehearsal-process-according-long-time-apple-designer/); [Inc. / Gallo, Bill Gates on Jobs](https://www.inc.com/carmine-gallo/steve-jobs-made-presentations-look-natural-you-can-too.html); [O'Reilly, *Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs*, Scene 15 "Make It Look Effortless"](https://oreilly.com/library/view/the-presentation-secrets/9780071636759/ch15.html)
- Caution: the often-quoted "100 hours" refers to app developers who shared the stage with Jobs ("100 hours of work for their two minutes"), not to Jobs himself. — [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/280205/enterprise-software-how-steve-jobs-beats-presentation-panic.html)

**John F. Kennedy and Ted Sorensen**
- Sorensen recorded Kennedy's preference for brevity. His drafting notes for the inaugural tell him to shorten sentences and words and to cut "I". — [JFK Library, "Analyzing the Rhetoric of JFK's Inaugural Address"](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/education/teachers/curricular-resources/analyzing-the-rhetoric-of-jfks-inaugural-address)
- Kennedy had Sorensen study the Gettysburg Address. Sorensen concluded that Lincoln favored small words and short phrases. — [JFK Library](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/education/teachers/curricular-resources/analyzing-the-rhetoric-of-jfks-inaugural-address); [Newsweek, "Ted Sorensen's Legacy for Writers"](https://newsweek.com/2010/11/02/ted-sorensen-s-legacy-for-writers.html)
- "Ask not what your country can do for you; ask what you can do for your country" is commonly taught as chiasmus. The Poetry Foundation notes that strict classical chiasmus inverts grammatical structure, so the line is better described as antimetabole. — [Poetry Foundation, "Governing Rhetoric"](https://poetryfoundation.org/articles/88761/governing-rhetoric)
- Sources give the inaugural's length as about 1,355 or 1,366 words. — [JFK Library](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/education/teachers/curricular-resources/analyzing-the-rhetoric-of-jfks-inaugural-address)
- Sorensen describes his process in his own words. — [Big Think: Ted Sorensen on Writing JFK's Speeches](https://bigthink.com/videos/ted-sorensen-on-writing-jfks-speeches/)

**Abraham Lincoln**
- He is the model text for the "short words" school: Kennedy's team analyzed the Gettysburg Address for its preference for one-syllable words and short phrases. — [JFK Library](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/education/teachers/curricular-resources/analyzing-the-rhetoric-of-jfks-inaugural-address)

**Barack Obama and Jon Favreau**
- Before most speeches, Obama met Favreau for about an hour to explain what he wanted to say. Favreau typed notes and wrote the first draft. — [NBC News, "Helping to write history"](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna28291755)
- For major addresses the process starts weeks or months ahead with "the download," in which the president gives the writer his ideas. — [Harvard Political Review interview with Favreau](https://harvardpolitics.com/jon-favreau-president-obamas-former-director-of-speechwriting)
- For the second inaugural, Favreau gathered "a binder full of speeches," Obama's own best addresses, and mined them for phrases and themes. The team also weaves in lines from Obama's books to keep his voice. — [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/obamas-inaugural-address-jon-favreau_n_2545236); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/politics/1108122/obamas-speechwriter-from-intern-to-top-wordsmith/)
- Favreau had memorized most of Obama's 2004 DNC keynote and carried *Dreams From My Father* with him. Absorbing the speaker's voice was his preparation as a writer. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna28291755)
- Obama wrote much of his own material, sometimes entire drafts. — [Newsweek, "Obama's Speechwriter Speaks Up"](https://europe.newsweek.com/obamas-speechwriter-speaks-87019)
- Favreau's advice is not to look for the "snappy soundbite" and to focus on the larger story. — [Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/?p=48063)

**Martin Luther King Jr.**
- The "I have a dream" passage was partly improvised. Mahalia Jackson called out "tell them about the dream," and King left his prepared text. — [NDLA, "A Rhetorical Analysis of I Have a Dream"](https://ndla.no/r/engelsk-2/a-rhetorical-analysis-of-i-have-a-dream/7259975f51); [PR Daily, "I Have a Dream holds 5 lessons for speechwriters"](https://www.prdaily.com/i-have-a-dream-holds-5-lessons-for-speechwriters/)
- The speech is built on anaphora: "I have a dream" (counts of eight or nine vary by source), "Now is the time" and "Let freedom ring." — [NDLA](https://ndla.no/r/engelsk-2/a-rhetorical-analysis-of-i-have-a-dream/7259975f51); [Shmoop, Anaphora](https://www.shmoop.com/study-guides/historical-texts/i-have-a-dream/anaphora-device.html)
- It is described as a sermon full of biblical language and imagery, which places it in the Black preaching tradition. — [PR Daily](https://www.prdaily.com/i-have-a-dream-holds-5-lessons-for-speechwriters/)
- Nancy Duarte's sparkline of the speech shows rapid movement between "what is" and "what could be," heavy repetition, and three crescendos where the audience applauded. — [Duarte, "The secret structure of great talks"](https://www.duarte.com/resources/secret-structure-great-talks/); [Manner of Speaking review of *Resonate*](https://mannerofspeaking.org/?p=5835)

**Sir Ken Robinson (TED)**
- "Do Schools Kill Creativity?" (2006) has been described as the most popular TED talk. View counts of more than 75 million are cited, but they conflict across uploads. — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/smart-skills/sir-ken-robinson/); [The Speaker Lab breakdown](https://thespeakerlab.com/blog/ken-robinson-ted-talk/)
- Annotated transcript: [James Clear, Great Speeches](https://jamesclear.com/great-speeches/do-schools-kill-creativity-by-ken-robinson)

### Inferences
- Development follows the same pattern across speakers: heavy input (reading, absorbing the voices of great speakers), then deliberate output (speaking practice, rehearsal out loud), then feedback (recordings, team review). Churchill's reading routine, Jobs's recorded rehearsals and Favreau's memorizing are versions of the same loop.
- Collaboration is the norm at the top. Kennedy/Sorensen and Obama/Favreau worked in co-authorship models, so a course can teach students to "build a writing partnership" or to self-edit in the same way.
- Improvisation works only on top of an internalized structure. King's improvised peroration drew on refrains he had used in earlier sermons, and Churchill ad-libbed between note sheets.

### Gaps
- Mandela, Brené Brown, Tony Robbins, Simon Sinek and Peggy Noonan: no sourced findings were collected in this pass, because of the tool budget. Do not cite claims about their methods from these notes.
- The full text of "The Scaffolding of Rhetoric" was not fetched (the host is blocked). The five elements listed above come from teaching sources and should be checked against the essay.
- Churchill's often-repeated "one hour of preparation per minute of speech" ratio was not confirmed in this pass.
- No primary source gives an exact count of Jobs's rehearsal hours.

## Q2. Israeli speakers and Israeli academic analyses of Hebrew rhetoric

### Takeaway
Israeli political oratory is heavily intertextual: Ben-Gurion cited biblical or rabbinic sources in 79% of a sample of his wartime speeches. Abba Eban is the archetype of the eloquent English-language diplomat-orator, and Begin of the emotional, honor-centered mass orator. A body of academic work exists on this (Landau, Nir, Hebrew University seminars, JPPI, Kesher), but systematic rhetorical studies of individual Hebrew speakers are thinner than English-language work.

### Cited Findings

**David Ben-Gurion**
- In a JPPI study of 33 speeches from 1948–49, Ben-Gurion cited biblical or rabbinic texts in 79% of them, compared with 58% for Netanyahu's wartime speeches (about 120 speeches in total). Netanyahu used formulas such as "with God's help" in 59% of speeches against Ben-Gurion's 21%. Ben-Gurion referred to Moses, Joshua, Bar Kokhba and the Hasmoneans in 42% of his speeches. — [JPPI study](https://jppi.org.il/en/first-israeli-pm-cited-the-bible-more-than-pm-netanyahu-in-wartime-speeches-study-finds/); [JPPI draft PDF](https://jppi.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PM-War-Speeches-Draft.pdf); [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/jewish-world/article/rjtqvwzrll)
- Hebrew University research on his Independence Day speeches examines how he used the Jewish past to frame the "birth" of the nation. — [HUJI CRIS: "The Jewish past and the 'birth' of the Israeli nation state"](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-jewish-past-and-the-birth-of-the-israeli-nation-state-the-cas-2/)
- Anita Shapira's article on Ben-Gurion and the Bible is a standard course reading. — [HUJI syllabus](https://shnaton.huji.ac.il/index.php/NewSyl/56021/1/2016/pdf)

**Menachem Begin**
- He learned his oratory at law school at the University of Warsaw. It became his trademark, and critics called it demagogy. — [Wikipedia mirror: Menachem Begin](https://bafybeiemxf5abjwjbikoz4mc3a3dla6ual3jsgpdr4cjr3oz3evfyavhwq.ipfs.4everland.io/wiki/Menahem_Begin.html)
- Recurring features: an "us and them" division (for example the 1952 Reparations speech), Jewish honor as a central motif, and heavy repetition of the message (the National Library notes this for his 15 May 1948 underground radio address). — [NLI blog: Begin swears allegiance](https://blog.nli.org.il/en/hoi_menachem_begin_speech/); [Tovia Preschel commentary](https://toviapreschel.com/?p=2929)
- A commentator contrasts his emotional Hebrew and Yiddish political oratory with his calm lecture tone. This is opinion, not a study. — [toviapreschel.com](https://toviapreschel.com/?p=2929)
- Ben-Gurion University catalogs a Hebrew publication on Begin as a leader, ideologue and theologian of history. — [BGU CRIS](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%97%D7%9D-%D7%91%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9F-%D7%9B%D7%9E%D7%A0%D7%94%D7%99%D7%92-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%93%D7%90%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%92-%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%91%D7%A9%D7%9D-%D7%AA%D7%90%D7%95%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%92%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%A9%D7%9C-%D7%94%D7%94%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%94-%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%94-/)

**Abba Eban**
- His speeches almost always combined strategic and moral appeals. In his 1949 UN admission speech he compared Israel to a man attacked by seven gangsters who then finds them sitting as his judges, an extended analogy in the Churchillian mode. — [Algemeiner, 70th anniversary of the Eban speech](https://www.algemeiner.com/2019/05/16/70th-anniversary-of-israels-admission-to-the-un-the-historic-abba-eban-speech/)
- His widow described a mellifluous voice and a gift for language. His "patrician" style made him more celebrated abroad than at home. — [J. Weekly](https://jweekly.com/2003/03/07/abba-eban-is-memorialized-friend-statesman-and-intellectual/); [Mosaic, "Who Was Abba Eban?"](https://mosaicmagazine.com/observation/2016/02/who-was-abba-eban/)
- A University of Birmingham study credits his oratory, linguistic skills and diplomatic style with shaping both American public opinion and official US–Israel relations (1950s–60s). — [Birmingham research portal](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/abba-eban-and-the-development-of-americanisraeli-relations-195019)
- Biography for further study: Asaf Siniver, *Abba Eban: A Biography*. — [Everand listing](https://www.everand.com/book/587272025/Abba-Eban-A-Biography)

**Yitzhak Rabin**
- For his Mount Scopus honorary-doctorate speech after the Six-Day War, Chief of Staff Rabin had the IDF's Chief Education Officer write it, and several General Staff members then vetted it. This is a documented example of a collaborative Israeli speech. — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-981-16-3579-3_2)

**Benjamin Netanyahu (rhetorical analysis only)**
- His March 2015 address to the US Congress has been analyzed as a "securitizing move" using classical rhetoric and securitization theory. — [Amin & Gagaridis 2019, via Academia/IJLS](https://www.academia.edu/43194933)
- A *Language and Dialogue* (2016) systemic-functional analysis of ten speeches to five international audiences looks at how he "recruits" the audience (the interpersonal strand). — [Benjamins](https://benjamins.com/online/ebop/publications/67395); [IUCC CRIS](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-interpersonal-strand-of-political-speech-recruiting-the-audie/)
- A University of Amsterdam-linked paper looks at Holocaust analogies in his 2009–15 speeches, and a 2023 HBKU thesis traces eight recurring themes. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/43194933); [HBKU thesis](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/studentTheses/an-analysis-of-themes-and-thematic-progression-patterns-in-benjam/)
- *Kesher* (Tel Aviv University) has an article on the rhetoric of Olmert and Netanyahu during criminal proceedings. — [TAU Kesher 57, Balas & Malkin](https://dacenter.tau.ac.il/sites/abraham.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/daniel%20abraham/kesher/57/4_Kesher57_Balas_Malkin.pdf)

**Israeli academic infrastructure on Hebrew political rhetoric**
- Standard references: Rachel Landau, *The Rhetoric of the Political Speech in Israel* (1986), and Raphael Nir on electoral rhetoric in Israel. Both appear as readings in a Hebrew University seminar on Israeli political rhetoric. — [HUJI syllabus 56021](https://shnaton.huji.ac.il/index.php/NewSyl/56021/1/2016/pdf); [HUJI syllabus 56983](https://shnaton.huji.ac.il/index.php/NewSyl/56983/1/2014/pdf)
- A computational study of "delegitimization discourse" in Israeli politics uses a Hebrew corpus of 10,410 sentences from Knesset speeches, Facebook posts and news. — [arXiv via AITopics](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:89531397)
- The Ministry of Education publishes Hebrew teaching material on speech analysis. — [Ministry of Education, neum-kalmanson PDF](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Pop/0files/ivrit_habaah_lashon/neum-kalmanson-13.8.pdf)

**Contemporary Israeli lecturers**
- Tal Ben-Shahar's Positive Psychology course (Psych 1504) became one of the most popular in Harvard's history, with about 855 to 900 students. The figure of more than 1,400 combines it with his Leadership course. — [NPR, "Finding Happiness in a Harvard Classroom"](https://www.npr.org/2006/03/22/5295168/finding-happiness-in-a-harvard-classroom); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Ben-Shahar); [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/Jewish-World/Jewish-Features/Harvard-classrooms-are-standing-room-only-for-popular-Israeli-professor)
- A University of Haifa study examined why his course worked ("Learning from success"). — [Haifa CRIS](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/learning-from-success-a-close-look-at-a-popular-positive-psycholo/); [Institute of Coaching summary](https://instituteofcoaching.org/resources/learning-success-close-look-popular-positive-psychology-course)
- Yuval Noah Harari is a Hebrew University historian (*Sapiens*, *Homo Deus*) and a TED speaker. — [TED speaker page](https://www.ted.com/speakers/yuval_noah_harari?language=en)
- Micah Goodman lectures at Israel's universities, think tanks and to political leaders. His talks pair modern problems with classical Jewish texts (for example "Modern Problems, Ancient Solutions"), and he teaches Deuteronomy as "the last speech of Moses." — [JPPI bio](https://jppi.org.il/en/?p=15825); [Pardes](https://elmad.pardes.org/els/2019/07/modern-problems-ancient-solutions/); [Tikvah course](https://ideas.tikvah.org/courses/deuteronomy-the-last-speech-of-moses/lesson/politics-and-power)

### Inferences
- A distinctly Hebrew persuasive resource is intertextuality: a single biblical phrase brings a whole narrative frame with it for a Hebrew-literate audience. Ben-Gurion's 79% citation rate suggests he relied on it as a core device. Students can learn to use biblical or liturgical "quotation fragments" deliberately.
- Begin and Eban show two Israeli archetypes: emotional, honor-and-identity oratory aimed at a home audience, and elegant, analogy-driven oratory aimed at international audiences. Their reception at home differed in the same way.
- The Goodman and Ben-Shahar model ("an ancient text or scientific finding applied to a personal modern problem") is a repeatable structure for lecturers.

### Gaps
- No sourced findings on Shimon Peres's style, Rabin's later speeches (including any role for Eitan Haber), Israeli TEDx stars, or Israeli motivational and business speakers.
- No academic source was found that catalogs Hebrew-specific devices (for example parallelism, smikhut, root-based wordplay) for persuasion. This needs a dedicated Hebrew-language search (for example on Raphael Nir, or Hebrew linguistics journals).
- No study directly comparing a leader's Hebrew and English speeches was found.

## Q3. Cross-cutting patterns and large-sample research (TED and famous speeches)

### Takeaway
Large-sample TED research points to visual, embodied delivery (especially "illustrator" gestures) and to the alternating structure Duarte describes. TED's curator puts rehearsal and a single throughline first. The evidence is correlational, and the most-cited gesture figures come from a non-peer-reviewed company study.

### Cited Findings
- Rizzo, Berger & Zhou (2025, *Journal of Marketing Research*) analyzed about 2,184 TED talks split into roughly 200,000 ten-second clips, labeled by AI. Only "illustrator" gestures, which depict the content, made speakers seem more knowledgeable and improved understanding. Generic "highlighter" or emphasis gestures and unrelated movement had little or no effect. Experiments with identical scripts confirmed the result. — [UBC News](https://news.ubc.ca/2025/11/talk-with-your-hands-to-be-more-persuasive/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1104598)
- Science of People (Vanessa Van Edwards, company study, not peer-reviewed): about 760 volunteers rated 2010 TED talks. The most popular talks averaged about 465 hand gestures in 18 minutes against about 272 for the least popular, with 7.36 million against 124,000 average views. Raters watching with the sound off judged charisma similarly to those watching with sound. — [Science of People, TED report](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/secrets-of-a-successful-ted-talk); [HubSpot coverage](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/ted-talk-public-speaking-study)
- Nancy Duarte, *Resonate*: great talks alternate between "what is" and "what could be" and end with a "new bliss" call to action. Examples include King, Feynman's gravity lecture, Benjamin Zander's TED talk and Jobs's 2007 iPhone launch. The number of speeches she analyzed is cited inconsistently ("dozens" to "250+"). — [Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/resources/secret-structure-great-talks/); [Manner of Speaking](https://mannerofspeaking.org/?p=5835)
- Chris Anderson (TED): shorter talks need proportionally more preparation (a 10-minute talk may take weeks). He recommends a throughline, warns against reading or using a teleprompter, and says the gap between owning a talk and being owned by it is rehearsal. — [Duarte blog on Anderson's advice](https://www.duarte.com/blog/10-ways-to-prepare-for-a-ted-format-talk/); [HBR reprint, "How to Give a Killer Presentation"](https://depts.washington.edu/dbpeds/How-To-Give-a-TED%20Talk%20Killer-Presentation%20(Harvard%20Biz%20Rvw).pdf); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/the-head-of-ted-says-too-many-people-skip-a-simple-but-crucial-step-before-giving-a-presentation/articleshow/52154001.cms); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/advice/ted-talks-curator-on-public-speaking/amp)
- Reportedly at least a third of the most-viewed TED talks use no slides. This comes from a secondary blog summary of Anderson's book. — [Medium](https://medium.com/improving-public-speaking-skills-in-the-workplace/rehearse-rehearse-rehearse-7e7104e0ea60)

### Inferences
Traits shared across Q1, Q2 and this section:
1. **Massive preparation that hides itself**: Jobs's two days of dress rehearsal, Churchill's notes, Anderson's ratio.
2. **Recorded feedback loops**: Jobs's team reviews.
3. **Absorbing models**: Churchill's reading, Favreau memorizing Obama, Kennedy studying Lincoln, Ben-Gurion's Bible.
4. **Repetition and refrain**: King, Begin.
5. **Contrast structures**: Duarte's what is / what could be, Kennedy's antimetabole.
6. **Extended analogy**: Eban's "seven gangsters," which matches Churchill's "analogy" element.
7. **Content-depicting gestures**: Rizzo et al.
8. **Planned improvisation**: King, Churchill.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on pause length or words-per-minute norms for elite speakers was gathered.
- No peer-reviewed study linking rehearsal hours to outcomes was found.

## Q4. Learnable lessons: a technique-by-technique breakdown with example lines

### Takeaway
Each technique below is tied to a documented example and can be drilled. The example lines are well-known public texts. Sources are the ones cited above.

### Cited Findings
| # | Technique | Example | Drill | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Anaphora / refrain | "I have a dream…", "Let freedom ring…" (King) | Write 4 to 8 parallel sentences opening with the same phrase and build intensity across them | [NDLA](https://ndla.no/r/engelsk-2/a-rhetorical-analysis-of-i-have-a-dream/7259975f51) |
| 2 | Antimetabole / chiasmus | "Ask not what your country can do for you; ask what you can do for your country" (JFK) | Invert one sentence's key terms ("A for B, not B for A") | [Poetry Foundation](https://poetryfoundation.org/articles/88761/governing-rhetoric) |
| 3 | Short Anglo-Saxon words | Gettysburg Address; Sorensen's "shorten sentences and words" notes | Rewrite a paragraph replacing every multi-syllable word you can | [JFK Library](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/education/teachers/curricular-resources/analyzing-the-rhetoric-of-jfks-inaugural-address) |
| 4 | Extended analogy | Eban: a man attacked by gangsters who then sit as his judges (1949 UN) | Explain your case as a single human-scale scene | [Algemeiner](https://www.algemeiner.com/2019/05/16/70th-anniversary-of-israels-admission-to-the-un-the-historic-abba-eban-speech/) |
| 5 | What-is / what-could-be alternation | King's sparkline, three crescendos | Map your talk on a sparkline and add at least three turns | [Duarte](https://www.duarte.com/resources/secret-structure-great-talks/) |
| 6 | Intertextual citation (Hebrew) | Ben-Gurion's biblical references (79% of speeches) | Anchor each major point in one biblical or classic phrase | [JPPI](https://jppi.org.il/en/first-israeli-pm-cited-the-bible-more-than-pm-netanyahu-in-wartime-speeches-study-finds/) |
| 7 | Illustrator gestures | TED analysis of about 2,184 talks | Choreograph a depicting gesture for each key concept (size, direction, timeline) | [UBC](https://news.ubc.ca/2025/11/talk-with-your-hands-to-be-more-persuasive/) |

### Inferences
- A course could be ordered as: input (absorbing models), then structure (throughline and sparkline), then language (short words, anaphora, antimetabole, analogy, intertextuality), then body (illustrator gestures), then rehearsal (recorded feedback loops), then improvisation on top of mastered refrains.
