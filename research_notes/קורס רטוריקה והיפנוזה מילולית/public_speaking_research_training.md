# Public Speaking, Rhetoric and Delivery: Evidence Base and Training Methods (as of Nov 2026)

Evidence grading legend: **STRONG** = meta-analyses / multiple RCTs / replicated; **MODERATE** = several controlled studies or one large well-designed study; **WEAK** = single small studies, correlational, observational or expert consensus; **DEBUNKED** = failed replication or misrepresented source.
Marker **[PK]** = citation drawn from researcher's prior knowledge with a canonical DOI/URL; it was not re-fetched in this session (two hosts, ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and frontiersin.org, were blocked by the session network), so the report writer should treat [PK] details as "verify before quoting exact numbers".

## 1. Classical rhetoric that still matters, and empirical tests of rhetorical devices

### Takeaway
The classical toolkit (ethos/pathos/logos, kairos, five canons) remains the best organizing scaffold for a course, and two devices have real empirical support as audience-response triggers in live political speech: the **contrast (antithesis)** and the **three-part list (tricolon)** (Atkinson; Heritage & Greatbatch). Most other figures (anaphora, chiasmus) are supported mainly by expert consensus and corpus/case analysis rather than controlled experiments.

### Cited Findings
- Aristotle's *Rhetoric* defines rhetoric as the faculty of observing the available means of persuasion and divides artistic proofs into ethos (speaker character), pathos (audience emotion) and logos (argument/demonstration); full text — [Aristotle, Rhetoric (MIT Classics)](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/rhetoric.html) [PK]. Evidence grade for the framework as a teaching scaffold: expert consensus (WEAK-as-empirical, but universal in curricula).
- The five canons (inventio, dispositio, elocutio, memoria, pronuntiatio/actio) are laid out systematically in Quintilian's *Institutio Oratoria* (and Cicero / *Rhetorica ad Herennium*) — [Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria (LacusCurtius, U. Chicago)](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Quintilian/Institutio_Oratoria/home.html) [PK].
- Max Atkinson coined "claptraps" in *Our Master's Voices* (1984) after analysing UK party-conference speeches; the **contrast** is by far the most frequent applause-inviting device; skilled use of contrasts and three-part lists is characteristic of speakers perceived as "charismatic"; neither device explicitly asks for applause — [Bull 2016, White Rose eprints](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf); [Max Atkinson blog, "rhetoric" label](https://maxatkinson.blogspot.com/search/label/rhetoric). Grade: **MODERATE** (large observational corpora, replicated across decades in UK; conversation-analytic, not experimental).
- Heritage & Greatbatch (1986) analysed all 476 speeches from 1981 televised British party conferences (Conservative, Labour, Liberal); a secondary summary reports contrasts accounted for ~33.2% and lists ~12.6% of collective applause (verify against original) — summary via [Bull 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf); original: [Heritage & Greatbatch, American Journal of Sociology 92(1), 1986](https://doi.org/10.1086/228465) [PK]. Grade: **MODERATE**.
- Atkinson-style analysis is culture-sensitive: in Japanese political speeches, *explicit* applause requests matter far more than in British ones — [Bull 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf).
- Peter Bull's later work extends Atkinson to audience responses beyond applause (laughter, cheering, booing) and compares e.g. Obama vs. Romney audience reactions — [BPS Research Digest: Obama vs Romney speech styles](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/comparing-obamas-and-romneys-speech-styles-and-way-their-audiences-react); ["Stirring it up!" Emotionality in audience responses to political speeches (Russian Journal of Linguistics)](https://journals.rudn.ru/linguistics/article/view/27475).
- Atkinson also analysed 2005 UK party leaders' conference speeches for the British Psychological Society — [The Psychologist: Leader speeches at 2005 conferences](https://bps.org.uk/psychologist/leader-speeches-2005-party-political-conferences).

### Inferences
- For course design: teach contrast and three-part lists early (beginner/intermediate) as highest-yield "applause/emphasis" devices; teach timing (delivery stress, pause before the final item, rising emphasis) as part of the device, since Atkinson's work concerns the vocal-plus-verbal package, not wording alone.
- Anaphora, chiasmus, epistrophe can be taught as "advanced style" with honest framing that their evidence is case analysis and expert consensus.
- Kairos (fitting the message to moment and audience) is consistent with the cultural-variation finding (Japan vs. UK): devices are not universal triggers.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment found in this session isolating anaphora/chiasmus/tricolon effects on persuasion or recall (some psycholinguistic "rhyme-as-reason" and fluency work exists but was not retrieved).
- Original Heritage & Greatbatch percentages not verified against the primary article.

## 2. Empirical research on delivery: voice, nonverbal behavior, and debunked claims

### Takeaway
Voice is the best-researched delivery channel: **wider pitch range, moderately higher pitch for men, somewhat faster (but not too fast) tempo, and varied rhythm** raise perceived charisma (Niebuhr and colleagues; Signorello), with clear inverted-U limits. Gesture and eye-contact evidence is thinner and more nuanced (eye contact can *reduce* persuasion among disagreeing listeners). The **Mehrabian 7-38-55 rule is DEBUNKED** as applied to speaking, and **power posing's hormonal/behavioral claims are DEBUNKED** (only small felt-power effects survive).

### Cited Findings
**Voice / acoustic charisma**
- Higher pitch is linked to higher charisma ratings for male speakers and lower pitch for female speakers; wider pitch range also helps — [Berger 2024 dissertation, Kiel University](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/macau_derivate_00006497/Berger_2024_Dissertation_final.pdf). Grade: **MODERATE**.
- Niebuhr & Skarnitzl analysed 51 speakers on which F0 level and range measures best predict perceived charisma, and sex differences — [Niebuhr & Skarnitzl 2019, Charles University](https://fonetika.ff.cuni.cz/wp-content/uploads/sites/104/2019/08/NieSka19-f0Charisma.pdf).
- Niebuhr, Tegtmeier & Schweisfurth (2019): higher F0 and faster rate increase perceived charisma, but beyond a point (too high / too fast) charisma drops drastically (inverted-U) — [Frontiers in Communication 2019](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00012/pdf). Grade: **MODERATE**.
- Niebuhr, Thumm & Michalsky: "high pitch" effects also depend on timing and shaping of pitch accents, not only F0 level — [Shapes and timing in charismatic speech](https://typeset.io/pdf/shapes-and-timing-in-charismatic-speech-evidence-from-sounds-m381ume00z.pdf).
- Niebuhr & Taghva (Interspeech 2024): duration variation of larger rhythm units correlates positively and gender-independently with charisma ratings (i.e., rhythmic variety, not monotone pacing) — [Interspeech 2024 listing](https://papers.cool/venue/niebuhr24@interspeech_2024@ISCA).
- Charisma can be assessed from ultra-short conversational clips via prosodic prominence (Linke, Niebuhr et al. 2026) — [Forum Acusticum 2026 paper](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2026/data/articles/000770.pdf). Grade: WEAK (single new study).
- Related Frontiers 2022 charisma/voice paper — [Frontiers in Communication 2022, fcomm.2022.909427](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.909427/pdf).
- Rosario Signorello (UCLA, later CNRS/Paris) separates a biological and a language/culture component of charismatic voice, using delexicalized speech; Umberto Bossi's post-stroke voice shifted follower perceptions from "authoritarian" to "benevolent," attributed to pitch contour changes; Italians appear to prefer a more dominant leader voice, the French a more competent one — [ScienceDaily 2014](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/10/141029203947.htm); [Phys.org 2014](https://phys.org/news/2014-10-science-charismatic-voices.html); [Acoustics.org: Charisma in bilingual English–Spanish politicians](https://acoustics.org/1psc26-acoustics-and-perception-of-charisma-in-bilingual-english-spanish-rosario-signorello/). Grade: WEAK–MODERATE (conference work, small speaker samples).
- D'Errico, Signorello, Demolin & Poggi, "The Perception of Charisma from Voice: A Cross-Cultural Study": pitch and pause types affect specific dimensions of perceived charisma, with cultural differences — referenced in [ScienceDaily 2014](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/10/141029203947.htm).

**Gestures and eye contact**
- Chen, Minson, Schöne & Heinrichs (2013, *Psychological Science*): more gaze at a speaker's eyes was associated with *less* attitude change; instructed eye contact produced less persuasion than looking at the mouth; eye contact helped only when listeners already agreed — [PubMed 24068114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24068114/); [Harvard Kennedy School summary](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/eye-beholder-eye-contact-increases-resistance-persuasion). Grade: **MODERATE** (controlled, but video-based, not live audiences — critique in [Throughline Group](https://www.throughlinegroup.com/?p=17312)).
- Rodero (2022, *Frontiers in Communication*) experimentally tested pitch variation × hand-gesture intensity in public presentations with 120 participants — [Frontiers fcomm.2022.869084](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.869084/pdf) (results not retrieved this session; host blocked).
- Popular TED-talk analysis (Van Edwards) found number of hand gestures correlated with talk popularity — reported in [Toastmasters Magazine, "The Power of Body Language" (2020)](https://abm.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2020/sept/the-power-of-body-language). Grade: WEAK (correlational, non-peer-reviewed).

**Debunked / misused**
- **Mehrabian 7-38-55: DEBUNKED as a general rule.** The ratio comes from two 1967 studies of inconsistent single-word messages about feelings/attitudes (one with ~30 women participants), with no real conversations; Mehrabian's own site states the equations do not apply unless the communicator is talking about feelings or attitudes; David Lapakko: research "widely misinterpreted" — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/the-learning-curve/the-7-38-55-rule-debunking-the-golden-ratio-of-conversation); [Fripp: Clarifying the 7-38-55 Rule](https://fripp.com/?p=33935); [Temple Law AER](https://law.temple.edu/aer/?p=3022).
- **Power posing: hormonal and behavioral claims DEBUNKED; felt-power effect small/contested.** Original Carney, Cuddy & Yap (2010) n=42; Ranehill et al. (2015) n≈200 found felt power but no hormone/behavior change; Ronay et al. (2017) direct replication found no significant effects; Simmons & Simonsohn's p-curve of 33 supporting studies was indistinguishable from zero effect plus selective reporting — [Simmons & Simonsohn, "Power Posing: P-Curving the Evidence" (UPenn)](https://repository.upenn.edu/fnce_papers/76); [PeerJ 2019: repeated power postures do not affect hormones](https://peerj.com/articles/6726); [Cambridge: powerful postures do not lead to risky behaviors](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8CB3B773548A4080BC0FE6985E1F7764/S1834490919000175a.pdf/powerful_postures_do_not_lead_to_risky_behaviors.pdf). Cuddy, Schultz & Fosse (2018) p-curve claims evidence for felt-power effects, criticized for confounding opposite-direction effects (Credé 2018) — summarized in [PeerJ 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6726); original [Psychological Science 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617746749) [PK]. Dana Carney publicly disavowed the effect in 2016 — [Carney statement (Berkeley Haas)](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/dana_carney/pdf_My%20position%20on%20power%20poses.pdf) [PK]. Gronau et al. (2017) Bayesian meta-analysis of 6 preregistered studies supports a felt-power effect of expansive vs. constrictive posture — per [PeerJ 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6726).

### Inferences
- Voice training targets with evidence: expand pitch range, emphasize key words with clear pitch accents, vary rhythm/phrase length, use purposeful pauses, run moderately brisk tempo—while avoiding extremes (inverted-U). This is measurable with free tools (e.g., Praat) and suits a drill-based curriculum.
- Teach "confident open posture" only as felt-confidence/comfort, never as hormone hacking.
- Eye contact: teach as connection with *supportive* audience members and as scanning; avoid "stare-down" persuasion advice for hostile audiences.
- Replace 7-38-55 with the accurate message: nonverbal/vocal cues dominate *when they conflict with words about feelings*; otherwise content matters greatly.

### Gaps
- Rodero 2022 effect sizes not retrieved. Live-audience experiments on gesture are few. No meta-analysis on speaker vocal cues and persuasion retrieved this session.

## 3. Structure, memory and "sticky" messages

### Takeaway
Structure guidance rests on robust cognitive psychology (serial-position / primacy-recency, cognitive load, concreteness/dual coding) plus popular syntheses (Heath brothers' SUCCESs). Direct speech-specific experiments were not retrieved this session; most structure advice is extrapolation from strong lab findings (grade: MODERATE when applied to speeches).

### Cited Findings
- Serial-position effect: items at beginning (primacy) and end (recency) of a list are recalled best — [Murdock 1962, J. Experimental Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045106) [PK]. Grade: **STRONG** in the lab; MODERATE as speech advice.
- Cognitive load theory: working memory is limited; instruction that overloads it impairs learning — [Sweller 1988, Cognitive Science](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4) [PK]. Grade: **STRONG** (education); supports few main points, signposting, and minimal slide text.
- Heath & Heath, *Made to Stick* (2007): sticky ideas are Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories (SUCCESs) — [Heath Brothers: Made to Stick](https://heathbrothers.com/books/made-to-stick/) [PK]. Grade: WEAK–MODERATE (synthesis of research + classroom exercises, not one experimental program).
- Atkinson's corpus work shows device placement and delivery timing matter for audience response (see §1) — [Bull 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf).

### Inferences
- Practical template: strong opening (primacy), one core message + max 3 supporting points (cognitive load; tricolon), concrete examples/stories, explicit signposts, repetition of the core message, deliberate close (recency). The "rule of three" is supported jointly by claptrap research and working-memory limits.

### Gaps
- No speech-specific RCT on openings/closings or repetition dose retrieved; storytelling-persuasion meta-analyses (narrative transportation) not retrieved this session.

## 4. Public speaking anxiety (PSA): prevalence and evidence-based treatment

### Takeaway
Psychological interventions reduce PSA with **medium-to-large effects (g ≈ 0.74 across 30 RCTs; g ≈ 1.17 in a 2025 meta-analysis)**; in vivo exposure is marginally better than VR exposure, but both are efficacious; technology-delivered treatment works about as well as face-to-face. Brief **anxiety reappraisal ("I am excited")** improved speech performance in Brooks (2014). Grade: **STRONG** for exposure/CBT; **MODERATE** for reappraisal.

### Cited Findings
- Ebrahimi et al. (2019, *Frontiers in Psychology*): 30 RCTs, 1,355 participants, 62 interventions; overall g = 0.74 (95% CI 0.61–0.87), low-to-moderate heterogeneity; technology-delivered ≈ face-to-face; cognitive/behavioral approaches showed continued improvement after treatment (sleeper effect); some indication of publication bias — [PMC6428748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6428748); [PubMed 30930813](https://www.pubmed.gov/30930813/).
- Reeves et al. (2022): 11 studies, 508 participants comparing VR exposure vs. in vivo exposure; both large effects, in vivo marginally superior — via search summary of [Frontiers / PubMed listings](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00488) (Reeves original not retrieved; verify).
- 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis (*The Journal of Psychology*): 26 studies, 2,253 participants, 42 interventions, overall g = 1.17, large variability, calls for long-term follow-up — [PubMed 39970240: "Review and Analysis of Successful PSA Interventions"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39970240/); [Bangor University record](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/portal/en/researchoutputs/review-and-analysis-of-successful-psa-interventions-an-applied-perspective(712c62bc-981a-4ca1-9624-f70b9dc72358).html).
- Brooks (2014, *J. Experimental Psychology: General* 143(3):1144–58): most people believe calming down is best, but saying "I am excited" (arousal-congruent reappraisal) improved performance in karaoke, public speaking and math; in the speech study the excited group spoke longer and was rated more persuasive, competent, confident and relaxed — [PubMed 24364682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/); [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2013/12/performance-anxiety). Grade: **MODERATE** (multiple experiments in one paper; independent replications mixed/not retrieved).
- Prevalence: Dwyer & Davidson (2012) challenge the "more feared than death" myth—public speaking is the most commonly *selected* fear on lists but death is rated more feared when ranked — [Communication Research Reports](https://doi.org/10.1080/08824096.2012.667772) [PK]. Bodie (2010) review of public speaking anxiety in Communication Education — [Bodie 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/03634520903443849) [PK]. (Commonly cited figures of ~15–30% high PSA in student samples should be verified before use.)

### Inferences
- Course design: build graded exposure into every stage (small low-stakes reps → larger audiences → VR/simulated hostile audiences), plus cognitive restructuring and a pre-speech "I'm excited" reappraisal script. Do not teach "just calm down" as the primary strategy.
- Sleeper effect suggests anxiety keeps dropping with continued practice after formal training—reinforces a long practice ladder.

### Gaps
- Exact current PSA prevalence estimates not verified this session. Reeves et al. 2022 primary text not fetched. Replication status of Brooks (2014) not checked.

## 5. How speaking expertise is built: deliberate practice, feedback, video, programs

### Takeaway
Deliberate practice (focused, feedback-rich, just-beyond-ability practice) is the right model, but its explanatory power is domain-dependent and contested (Macnamara 2014). Speech-specific training studies show **video feedback and structured behavioral skills training improve delivery**, but rigorous RCTs are few. Programs like Toastmasters and university courses (Stanford GSB etc.) embody these principles but have little published outcome evidence. Grade: **MODERATE** for feedback/video; **WEAK** for specific program brands.

### Cited Findings
- Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993) introduced deliberate practice as the driver of expert performance — [Psychological Review 100(3)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363) [PK].
- Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014) meta-analysis: deliberate practice explained 26% of performance variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, <1% professions — [Stafforini summary](https://stafforini.com/works/macnamara-2014-deliberate-practice-and/). Critics argue its definition of deliberate practice was too broad; a reanalysis found practice explained more variance (29–61% after attenuation correction) — [Progress-Focused critique](https://progressfocused.com/2014/11/macnamara-et-al-2014-meta-analysis-on.html); [Progress-Focused 2020](https://progressfocused.com/2020/08/confirmation-of-importance-of.html); [Frontiers in Psychology 2019 review](https://frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396/full). Grade: debate unresolved; practice clearly matters, magnitude contested.
- Liu & Aryadoust (quasi-experimental, EFL learners): video feedback significantly improved delivery skill and global competence and reduced speaking anxiety vs. verbal feedback only — [SiLA/ALTAANZ](https://www.altaanz.org/uploads/5/9/0/8/5908292/2._sila_11_2__liu_aryadoust.pdf). Grade: MODERATE–WEAK.
- Remote video-based Behavioral Skills Training (Laske et al., JABA): increased targeted public speaking behaviors in all participants (n=4, multiple-baseline); awareness training reduced disfluencies in a follow-up — [Behaviorist Book Club summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/practitioner-development/the-efficacy-of-remote-video-based-training-on-public-speaking/); [follow-up](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/practitioner-development/the-efficacy-of-remote-video-based-behavioral-skills-training-and-awar/). Grade: WEAK (tiny n) but mechanism-consistent.
- Randomized wait-list trial of speech training with 61 children: "partly successful" — [CAS catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,37862574).
- Undergraduates rated video feedback more useful than peer feedback (perceptions only) — [Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/36294912).
- Virtual-audience training: both real-time and delayed feedback components contribute benefits (Chollet et al. 2022) — per search summary; AI "self-clone" presentation training improved self-perception and skills — [arXiv 2310.15112](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.15112). Grade: WEAK–MODERATE.
- Toastmasters Pathways uses peer evaluation, repeated speeches and role rotation — [Toastmasters Pathways overview](https://www.toastmasters.org/pathways-overview) [PK]. Grade: WEAK (no independent outcome RCTs found).
- Stanford GSB lecturer Matt Abrahams teaches spontaneous speaking (improv-based "Think Faster, Talk Smarter") — [Stanford GSB faculty page](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/faculty/matt-abrahams) [PK]. Grade: expert consensus.

### Inferences
- Effective training loop for the course: short speech → video recording → targeted feedback on 1–2 measurable behaviors (e.g., filler rate, pitch range, pause use) → immediate re-do → spaced repetition. This combines deliberate-practice principles with the best-supported speech-specific intervention (video + specific feedback).
- Elite stage: coaching by experts, analysis of master speakers (Atkinson-style transcript/audio analysis), high-stakes simulations, and acoustic measurement against charisma parameters.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed outcome evaluations found of Toastmasters, Harvard, Northwestern or Wharton speaking programs; improv-training outcome studies not retrieved this session.

## 6. Israeli academic work on rhetoric, argumentation and political speech

### Takeaway
Israel's most internationally cited figure is **Ruth Amossy (Tel Aviv University, emerita; ADARR research group)**, author of the "argumentation in discourse" approach that fuses Perelman's New Rhetoric with French discourse analysis. Hebrew University and Bar-Ilan host political-rhetoric seminars and research on leaders' speeches and delegitimization discourse; Reichman-specific rhetoric faculty could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Amossy's "argumentation in discourse" joins Perelman's New Rhetoric with French discourse analysis; argumentation must be studied in its actual wording, context and doxa; descriptive/empirical rather than normative; discourse carries a general "argumentativity" even without explicit controversy — [OSSA 2009 keynote, "Argumentation in Discourse: A Socio-discursive Approach to Arguments"](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/doaj.art-6663544cbca44859add5b33362674bb7); [Argumentation et Analyse du Discours (journal), 2015](https://doaj.org/article/b42206872a244dc8b24cfee76e1cd59e); [Amossy, Argumentação e Análise do Discurso (USP PDF)](https://edisciplinas.usp.br/pluginfile.php/4864523/mod_resource/content/1/Amossy%2C%20R.%20Argumenta%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20e%20An%C3%A1lise%20do%20Discurso%20perspectivas%2C%20teorias%20e%20recortes%20disciplinares.pdf); [Amossy Medellín lecture](https://spapps-f1b66628637879.spapps.eafit.edu.co/cec/congresos/SiteAssets/Paginas/congresoiberoamericanoargumentacion/RUTH-AMOSSY-MEDELLIN.pdf). Amossy is also known for work on ethos (self-presentation in discourse) and polemics.
- Hebrew University: MA seminar "Political Rhetoric in Israel" (Political Science) analysing Israeli leaders' speeches — [HUJI syllabus](https://shnaton.huji.ac.il/index.php/NewSyl/56983/2/2014); Rivlin-Angert & Mor-Lan (EMNLP 2025) computational study of delegitimization discourse in ~10,000 Knesset sentences — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/emnlp/2025.emnlp-main.841.pdf); Zohar Kampf & Meital Balmas on leaders' conflict-related messaging — [International Journal of Communication 11 (2017)](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/4658/1995).
- Bar-Ilan: Gilad Greenwald (School of Communication, political communication) — [BIU page](https://sites.biu.ac.il/gilad-greenwald/page/10097); Elie Friedman designed and taught a BA course "Rhetoric and Public Speaking" at Bar-Ilan — [CV](https://ric.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Elie-Friedman-Academic-CV.pdf); Orly Kayam (PhD Hebrew Language and Rhetoric, Bar-Ilan; Wingate) on rhetorical strategies of Iranian presidents' UN speeches — [CV](https://www.wincol.ac.il/wincol.ac.il/originals/OrlyNEWCV2016.docx.pdf).
- Tel Aviv University humanities theses on rhetoric/culture also appear — [TAU abstract (Nadia Elis)](https://humanities.tau.ac.il/sites/humanities.tau.ac.il/files/media_server/Culture%20School/Files/נדיה%20אליס%20-%20תקציר%20באנגלית.pdf).

### Inferences
- An Israeli-flavored course can anchor its "invention/argumentation" module in Amossy (ethos in discourse, doxa, polemics) and use Knesset/leader speeches (Ben-Gurion, Begin, Rabin, Netanyahu) as case material, following HUJI/Bar-Ilan analytic models.

### Gaps
- Reichman University (IDC) rhetoric/public speaking faculty and current course rosters not confirmed. Hebrew-language rhetoric scholarship (e.g., Hebrew linguistics departments) not systematically surveyed. No Israeli empirical delivery/anxiety training studies retrieved.

## 7. Concrete drills with evidence or strong expert consensus

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced drills are: graded exposure reps, video self-review with specific feedback, arousal reappraisal, acoustic/prosody range drills, and device-timing drills modeled on claptrap research. Others are expert consensus.

### Cited Findings (drill → basis)
- **Graded exposure ladder / VR audience practice** → PSA meta-analyses (STRONG) — [Ebrahimi 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6428748); [PubMed 39970240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39970240/).
- **"I am excited" pre-speech reappraisal (say aloud)** → MODERATE — [Brooks 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/).
- **Record → review → targeted feedback → redo** → MODERATE — [Liu & Aryadoust](https://www.altaanz.org/uploads/5/9/0/8/5908292/2._sila_11_2__liu_aryadoust.pdf); BST studies [Behaviorist Book Club](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/practitioner-development/the-efficacy-of-remote-video-based-training-on-public-speaking/).
- **Filler/disfluency awareness training (count, then replace with pause)** → WEAK-MODERATE — [awareness training follow-up](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/practitioner-development/the-efficacy-of-remote-video-based-behavioral-skills-training-and-awar/).
- **Pitch-range and pitch-accent drills; tempo calibration within limits** → MODERATE — [Niebuhr et al. 2019](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00012/pdf); [Berger 2024](https://macau.uni-kiel.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/macau_derivate_00006497/Berger_2024_Dissertation_final.pdf).
- **Rhythm variation (vary phrase/clause lengths)** → WEAK-MODERATE — [Niebuhr & Taghva 2024](https://papers.cool/venue/niebuhr24@interspeech_2024@ISCA).
- **Contrast and three-part-list writing + delivery timing** → MODERATE — [Bull 2016 on Atkinson](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/100468/3/A_PublishedVersion.pdf).
- **SUCCESs rewrite (make an idea concrete, unexpected, story-based)** → WEAK-MODERATE — [Made to Stick](https://heathbrothers.com/books/made-to-stick/) [PK].
- **Improv/spontaneous speaking drills** → expert consensus — [Stanford GSB, Matt Abrahams](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/faculty/matt-abrahams) [PK].
- **Avoid**: power-pose hormone rituals (DEBUNKED) [Simmons & Simonsohn](https://repository.upenn.edu/fnce_papers/76); teaching 7-38-55 (DEBUNKED) [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/the-learning-curve/the-7-38-55-rule-debunking-the-golden-ratio-of-conversation).

### Inferences
- Suggested staging: Beginner = exposure + structure (3 points, opening/close) + reappraisal; Intermediate = video feedback loops, fillers/pauses, contrast/tricolon; Advanced = prosody/charisma parameters, storytelling, Q&A/improv; Elite = audience adaptation (kairos/culture), argument analysis (Amossy), high-stakes simulation and master-speaker analysis.

### Gaps
- No controlled trials found for many popular drills (mirror practice, tongue twisters, "ums" penalty games); these should be labeled expert-consensus only.
